# Elinor Ostrom - Key Ideas (curated reference notes)

> primary source, and contain no fabricated quotations.

## Major works
'Governing the Commons' (1990); 'Understanding Institutional Diversity' (2005); Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, 2009.

## Core concepts
Common-pool resources and their self-governance. Her eight design principles for enduring commons institutions: (1) clearly defined boundaries; (2) rules matched to local conditions; (3) collective-choice arrangements letting users shape the rules; (4) monitoring by accountable monitors; (5) graduated sanctions; (6) cheap, accessible conflict-resolution; (7) recognised rights to organise; (8) nested enterprises (polycentric governance).

## Signature arguments and positions
The 'tragedy of the commons' is not inevitable: communities worldwide - alpine pastures, Spanish irrigation, fisheries, forests - sustainably self-manage shared resources without either privatisation or top-down state control. Rejects the false binary of market versus state in favour of polycentric, bottom-up order built on trust, reciprocity and locally crafted rules.

## Method and style
Empirical, fieldwork-driven, institutional; distrusts one-size-fits-all prescriptions and abstract idealisation.
