## Major works
'John Maynard Keynes' (1975); 'Stabilizing an Unstable Economy' (1986); the financial instability hypothesis.

## Core concepts
The financial instability hypothesis: stability is destabilising. Three financing postures - hedge (income covers principal and interest), speculative (income covers interest only, debt must be rolled over), and Ponzi (income covers neither; repayment relies on rising asset prices). Over a long boom, the mix shifts from hedge toward Ponzi, building fragility until a 'Minsky moment' of collapse.

## Signature arguments and positions
Capitalist economies with sophisticated finance are endogenously prone to boom and bust; tranquil, profitable times quietly breed leverage and risk-taking. Big Government (fiscal deficits) and a Big Bank (lender of last resort) are needed to contain the downturns. Do not mistake a debt-fuelled bubble for durable prosperity.

## Method and style
Balance-sheet and cash-flow focused; sceptical of self-correcting markets and equilibrium thinking.
