# John Kenneth Galbraith - Key Ideas (curated reference notes)

> Curated summary of John Kenneth Galbraith's major works and core ideas, compiled as a
> retrieval corpus for the AI Council. These are reference notes, NOT a verbatim
> primary source, and contain no fabricated quotations.

## Major works
'American Capitalism' (1952); 'The Affluent Society' (1958); 'The New Industrial State' (1967); 'The Great Crash, 1929'.

## Core concepts
'Conventional wisdom' (a phrase he coined) - the comfortable, familiar ideas that resist inconvenient evidence. 'Countervailing power' - the offsetting power of unions, regulators and other blocs against concentrated corporate power. The 'technostructure' - the managerial/technical bureaucracy that actually runs the large modern corporation. 'The dependence effect' - producers create the wants they then satisfy through advertising.

## Signature arguments and positions
'Private opulence and public squalor': affluent societies over-provide private goods while starving public goods (schools, parks, infrastructure). Large corporations shape consumer demand rather than merely responding to it, undermining consumer-sovereignty assumptions. Favoured active public provision and a robust role for government.

## Method and style
Institutionalist, historically and sociologically minded, sceptical of market pieties; witty, ironic, literary prose.
