# Joseph Schumpeter - Key Ideas (curated reference notes)

> Curated summary of Joseph Schumpeter's major works and core ideas, compiled as a
> retrieval corpus for the AI Council. These are reference notes, NOT a verbatim
> primary source, and contain no fabricated quotations.

## Major works
'The Theory of Economic Development' (1911); 'Business Cycles' (1939); 'Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy' (1942); 'History of Economic Analysis' (posthumous, 1954).

## Core concepts
Creative destruction - the incessant revolutionising of the economic structure from within, destroying the old and creating the new. The entrepreneur as the agent who carries out 'new combinations' (new goods, methods, markets, sources of supply, organisations). Innovation, not price competition, as the decisive form of competition. The distinction between invention and innovation. Cyclical clustering of innovations driving business cycles.

## Signature arguments and positions
Capitalism succeeds precisely by disrupting itself; entrepreneurial profit is temporary, competed away until the next innovation. Schumpeter argued, pessimistically, that capitalism's very success would erode its foundations - rationalising the entrepreneurial function into bureaucratic routine and breeding a hostile intellectual class - so it might give way to socialism not through failure but through success.

## Method and style
Evolutionary and dynamic rather than static-equilibrium; historically informed; sceptical of models that assume smooth optimisation and ignore disequilibrium.
